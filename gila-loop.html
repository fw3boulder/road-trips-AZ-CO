Caves, Copper & Calderas

Homes in a canyon cave, towns built on silver, a city of volcanic stone, and a walkway hung in a slot canyon.

Why go

Southwest New Mexico was built by volcanoes. One collapsed 28 million years ago and left a 15-mile bowl where the Gila Cliff Dwellings now sit; another made the stone towers of City of Rocks. People followed: Mogollon families in the caves, then miners after silver and copper. Unpack once in Silver City and see all of it on three loops, then go home by a canyon walkway and a jail cut into a cliff.

Midday

Tucson to Shakespeare · 156 mi

Tour

🏚Shakespeare

It began as a stage stop, boomed on silver as Ralston, and collapsed after a diamond swindle was exposed in 1872. Seven years later it tried again under a grander name: Shakespeare. The railroad missed it and the 1893 crash finished it. The Hill family bought the whole town in 1935, and the tour goes inside eight buildings.

Guided tours only: 10, noon and 3, if a guide is available. $15 each, plus tax. Listed as open daily, but another listing says limited days, so open days are not confirmed: call 575-542-9034. Road surface at the gate not confirmed. I-10 can close for blowing dust near Lordsburg.

Afternoon

Shakespeare to Silver City · 47 mi

A stroll

🌊Silver City's lost Main Street

An 1895 flood dug a gorge straight down Main Street. The town simply turned around and made Bullard Street its center; the gorge is now Big Ditch Park. An 1880 law had required brick, which is why so much of the old downtown still stands.

Tonight

Silver City

Brick blocks, murals and hills on every side; three nights here, so unpack. One hotel is a sight in itself: the Murray, an Art Deco hotel from 1938 right downtown (looked up in a 2022 article; not confirmed open).

Morning

Silver City to the cliff dwellings · 42 mi

🛣NM 15, over the mountains

The first 25 miles are narrow and curling, with grades of 10 to 12 percent and no center line. Take it slowly and it is the best part of the drive.

Fill the tank first. No phone signal past Silver City or Mimbres. Not plowed at night or on weekends: check nmroads.com in winter. The easier way both ways is NM 152 and NM 35.

Two hours

🏺Gila Cliff Dwellings

The Mogollon built about fifty rooms inside these caves in the late 1270s and were gone in a generation. Below the cliff dwellings is the floor of a volcano: the land fell in about 28 million years ago and left a bowl 15 miles across. You walk right through the rooms.

1-mile loop, 180 ft up and down on uneven stone stairs with no handrails; 1 to 1½ hours. Ladders: not confirmed, ask at the trailhead. 9 to 3:59, off the trail by 4:50. Free. Closed Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's Day. Ice possible in winter. Only plain water on the trail.

Afternoon

Back by Lake Roberts and Mimbres · 72 mi

Short stops

🌲Lake Roberts and the Mimbres valley

A small lake in the pines, then down a quiet river valley. Mimbres people farmed here long before the ranches.

An August 2026 alert warned of harmful algae in Lake Roberts: look, don't wade. The Mimbres Culture Heritage Site has a short path at a ruin; hours not confirmed, 575-536-3333.

Dinner

🎭Pinos Altos and the Buckhorn

One of the few dinners that counts as a sight: a saloon and opera house that date to the 1860s gold camp, with a thick oak bar, brick walls and a stage with box seats. It is seven miles above Silver City.

Monday to Saturday from 3, food from 4, dining room from 5; closed Sunday. Book the dining room by phone: 575-538-9911.

Morning

Silver City to Hillsboro · about 65 mi

15 minutes

⛏Chino Mine overlook

Mining here went open-pit in 1910. The hole is now about 1¾ miles across and 1,350 feet deep. Driving in, look for the stone spire called the Kneeling Nun.

South side of NM 152, east of Hanover. Open now: not confirmed.

Views

🏔Emory Pass

The vista, above 8,000 feet, looks over Caballo Lake to mountains more than 50 miles east. Then the road drops nearly 3,000 feet in nine miles to Kingston, an 1880s mining camp.

Check nmroads.com for snow. Kingston's schoolhouse museum is by appointment: 575-895-5501.

Lunch and the museum

🐎Hillsboro

Cottonwoods, old adobes, and the Black Range Museum, in what was the hotel of Sadie Orchard. Its prize came home in fall 2025: the Mountain Pride, the stagecoach that ran between Lake Valley, Hillsboro and Kingston from 1889 to 1915.

Museum Friday 12 to 3, Saturday 10 to 3, Sunday 12 to 3; hours can vary: 575-895-5158.

Afternoon

Lake Valley and City of Rocks · about 112 mi

An hour

🏚Lake Valley

In 1881 miners opened a cave lined with glittering silver, nearly 200 feet long, and called it the Bridal Chamber. It gave up 2.5 million ounces. Fire took Main Street before dawn in 1895; the last residents left in 1994. You can go inside the schoolhouse and the chapel.

Thursday to Monday, 9 to 4, free. The self-guided walk takes about 45 minutes. Hail damaged buildings in October 2026 (broken glass; repairs under way). Last bit of road: surface not confirmed.

An hour

🌋City of Rocks State Park

A volcano in the mountains you just crossed blew up about 34.9 million years ago. Its ash welded into stone, and weather cut that into towers up to 40 feet high, with lanes between them like streets.

Wander as little or as much as you like; 5½ miles of trail in all. Hours and fee not confirmed.

Morning

Silver City to the Catwalk · 65 mi

An hour and a quarter

🌉The Catwalk

Whitewater Canyon pinches down to a slot, and the trail hangs in it on a steel walkway over the creek. A mill town stood here in the 1890s and lasted about ten years; the Civilian Conservation Corps rebuilt the path in the 1930s.

¾ mile each way; stairs not confirmed. Dawn to dusk, $5 a car in the self-service envelope. A Nov 23, 2026 order closes the trail beyond it for flood damage. The Catwalk isn't named in it; open is not confirmed: 575-388-8201. Low crossings on NM 174 can flood.

Afternoon

Over to Arizona and home · 234 mi

Half an hour

🔒Clifton's cliff jail

In 1878 Margarito Varela blasted two cells into the rock face, and the town says he was later locked up in them himself. A flood filled them with mud in 1906. The little Copperhead locomotive stands next door, and historic Chase Creek Street is nearby.

Going inside the jail: not confirmed.

A break

🔭Safford

Roper Lake State Park is a blue lake under Mount Graham. If you are in no hurry on a Saturday, Discovery Park opens at 4: a 20-inch telescope, free in the evening, and what it says is the world's largest camera obscura.

Roper Lake: 6 am to 8 pm, $20 a car for two to four people. Discovery Park: Saturday 4 to 9, hours may vary. It is still over two hours to Tucson.
