Castles & Canyons

A castle in a cliff, pueblos on the plateau, pine country in between, and home through the Salt River Canyon.

Why go

Skip the freeway through Phoenix: take the Pinal Pioneer Parkway and slip around on Loop 101. Two pueblos in the Verde Valley, a night in Old Town Cottonwood, then up over the rim through the pines to Route 66 and an ancestral Hopi village. The last day saves the best drive for the way home: the long switchbacks of the Salt River Canyon.

Morning

Tucson to Montezuma Castle · 230 mi

🌵Pinal Pioneer Parkway

Highway 79 was named a scenic parkway in 1961, with a wide strip of desert kept on both sides. Beside the road a riderless steel horse marks where the cowboy film star Tom Mix died in a 1940 car crash. There are shaded picnic tables.

About an hour

🏺Montezuma Castle

Look up. The Sinagua built this cliff home between about 1125 and 1173, high above Beaver Creek, and it still has its walls and windows. The name is all wrong: the place stood empty decades before Montezuma was born. Theodore Roosevelt made it one of the country's first national monuments in 1906.

A third-of-a-mile loop. You see the dwelling from below; visitors stopped climbing up in 1951. 9 to 4:45 daily, closed Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day. $10 each, good at Tuzigoot too; no cash.

Afternoon

Through the Verde Valley · 26 mi

About an hour

🏺Tuzigoot

At the Castle you look up; here you walk among the rooms. About 110 of them cover a ridge 120 feet above the Verde River, dug out in the 1930s as a Depression-era jobs project on land the county sold for a dollar.

Pueblo Trail: a 0.3-mile loop with steep grades and very little shade, 30 to 45 minutes. 9 to 5, closed the same holidays.

Tonight

Old Town Cottonwood

One compact main street on the Verde River side of the valley, made for a stroll: tasting rooms, galleries, antique shops and dinner, with hotels right in the middle of it.

Cottonwood to Winslow · 129 mi

Half an hour

🏫Strawberry Schoolhouse

Pioneer families built this one-room school of pine logs in 1885. It is a handsome thing to walk around even when the door is locked.

Tours mid-May to mid-October, on limited days; an outside look the rest of the year.

🌲Highway 87

North through miles of tall pines on the plateau above the rim, until the trees give way to open high desert. Check for snow in winter.

Midday

Winslow

Lunch

🚂La Posada

Fred Harvey's last great railroad hotel opened on Route 66 in 1930, designed by Mary Colter. Have lunch at the Turquoise Room.

Lunch listed daily 11 to 2, lighter menu 2 to 4 (not confirmed: the page has no date); reservations suggested.

Afternoon

Homolovi and on to Snowflake · 73 mi

An hour and a half

🏺Homolovi

The Hopi name means Place of the Little Hills. Ancestral Hopi people lived here in the 1300s, and Homolovi II, the biggest village in the park, had an estimated 1,200 to 2,000 rooms. At Tsu'vö, a half-mile loop between twin buttes, there are petroglyphs and grinding places.

Daily 8 to 5, shorter on Thanksgiving and Christmas Eve, closed Christmas. $20 a car for two to four people. Homolovi I is closed for erosion damage. Homolovi II (short paved trail): not confirmed open; ask at the visitor center.

Tonight

Snowflake

Nothing to do with the weather: Erastus Snow and William Flake founded the town in 1878. Brick pioneer houses still line the old streets, good for an evening walk.
